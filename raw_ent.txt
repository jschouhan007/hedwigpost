The Most Anticipated Comeback in Music History
On March 21, 2026, BTS made history for the second time in a single weekend — following the release of their new album Arirang on March 20, the group performed their first full concert in nearly four years at Gwanghwamun Square in central Seoul, South Korea. An estimated 260,000 fans packed the historic square — the largest gathering in the city for a K-pop concert ever recorded. The event was simultaneously live-streamed on Netflix to viewers in 190 countries, making it the first time the streaming giant had globally broadcast a live concert.
For BTS fans, known worldwide as ARMY, the weekend was the culmination of three and a half years of waiting. In 2022, BTS announced an indefinite hiatus as its members — RM, Jin, SUGA, j-hope, Jimin, V, and Jung Kook — began fulfilling South Korea's mandatory military service requirement. The final member, SUGA, completed his service in June 2025. The group had quietly reunited in a Los Angeles recording studio shortly after, spending July to November 2025 recording what would become Arirang.
Arirang: The Album That Reclaims Their Roots
Titled after Korea's most beloved traditional folk song, Arirang is BTS's fifth Korean-language studio album and their tenth album overall. The 14-track project is widely being described by critics as BTS's most artistically ambitious work since their 2014 breakthrough album Dark & Wild. Rather than chasing the glossy pop crossover sound of chart-toppers like Dynamite and Butter, Arirang leans into hip-hop, spoken word, and experimental sounds that reflect the group's personal growth during their years of military service and separation.
The album sold 3.98 million copies on its very first day — one of the highest first-day sales figures in K-pop history. The music video for the lead single SWIM surpassed 40 million views within 24 hours of release. On the album's first day, BTS's entire back catalogue re-entered streaming charts worldwide as new and returning listeners dove back in. Billboard confirmed the group's first-day numbers as among the highest for any album released in 2026.
"The opening 15 minutes of Arirang have the rebellious, rap-heavy energy of the band's 2014 album, Dark & Wild," wrote the BBC's Mark Savage in a glowing four-star review. "Surefire radio hits, they cemented BTS as the biggest K-pop band on Planet Earth. The good news is the band have rekindled that fire."
The Concert: 260,000 Fans and a Drone Light Show
The one-hour live show, officially titled BTS THE COMEBACK LIVE | ARIRANG, opened with a pre-show drone light show that illuminated the night sky above Seoul with portraits of the seven members. Directed by Hamish Hamilton — the British director responsible for multiple Super Bowl halftime shows and the Oscars — the stage design was built around the concept of a picture frame, set against the backdrop of Gyeongbokgung Palace. The setlist was dominated by new material from Arirang, including the anthemic closing number Into the Sun, during which all seven members shared vocal lines in a display of unity that reduced fans both at the venue and watching online to tears.
Jung Kook's high notes in "Please," Jimin's emotive delivery in "Merry Go Round," and SUGA and j-hope's rap intensity in "Hooligan" were cited by reviewers as the standout individual moments. RM performed despite an ankle injury that had concerned fans in the days leading up to the show. The concert was directed to be streamed on Netflix and will remain available for replay for all subscribers.

Best Picture Goes to "One Battle After Another"
Paul Thomas Anderson's "One Battle After Another" took home the biggest prize at the 98th Academy Awards on March 15, 2026, winning Best Picture in what was ultimately a dramatic six-award sweep. The film — a political thriller set in a police state version of America — also won Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Film Editing for Anderson, plus Best Supporting Actor for Sean Penn (who famously did not attend) and the first-ever Oscar for Best Casting, awarded to Cassandra Kulukundis. Anderson, receiving his first Oscar wins after more than 30 years of acclaimed filmmaking, thanked his children during his screenplay acceptance speech: "I wrote this movie for my kids to say sorry for the housekeeping mess that we left in this world we're handing off to them."
"Sinners" Made History — Just Not the Kind Anyone Expected
Ryan Coogler's Sinners arrived at the 98th Oscars carrying the most nominations in Academy Awards history — a staggering 16 nods, surpassing the previous records held by Titanic, All About Eve, and La La Land. It left with four Oscars — Best Actor for Michael B. Jordan, Best Original Screenplay for Coogler, Best Original Score for Ludwig Göransson, and Best Cinematography for Autumn Durald Arkapaw. Arkapaw's win was historic: she became the first woman ever to win the Academy Award for Best Cinematography.
The twelve losses mean Sinners now holds a different record — the most nominations to losses in Oscar history. The previous record of 11 losses was shared by five films going back to 1948. For Coogler, who coaxed his entire cast to stand for applause when he collected his screenplay award, the four wins clearly meant something deep. Michael B. Jordan, accepting his first Oscar for playing twins Smoke and Stack, recited from memory a list of previous Black Best Actor winners, honouring the "ancestors" whose legacy he was proud to extend.

Best Picture: One Battle After Another. Best Director: Paul Thomas Anderson (One Battle After Another). Best Actor: Michael B. Jordan (Sinners). Best Actress: Jessie Buckley (Hamnet). Best Supporting Actor: Sean Penn (One Battle After Another). Best Supporting Actress: Amy Madigan (Weapons). Best Animated Feature: KPop Demon Hunters. Best Original Song: "Golden" from KPop Demon Hunters — the first K-pop song ever to win an Oscar. Best International Feature Film: Sentimental Value (Norway). Best Documentary Feature: Mr. Nobody Against Putin. Best Original Screenplay: Ryan Coogler for Sinners. Best Adapted Screenplay: Paul Thomas Anderson for One Battle After Another. Best Original Score: Ludwig Göransson for Sinners. Best Cinematography: Sinners (Autumn Durald Arkapaw — first woman ever). Best Film Editing: One Battle After Another (Andy Jurgensen). Best Casting (inaugural award): Cassandra Kulukundis for One Battle After Another. Best Live Action Short Film: Tie — "The Singers" and "Two People Exchanging Saliva" — only the seventh tie in Oscars history.
The Ceremony: Political, Emotional, and Occasionally Chaotic
Conan O'Brien returned as host for the second consecutive year, opening with an extended video parody that saw him chased through scenes from Oscar-nominated films by a group of schoolchildren. The ceremony was held under unusually heavy security — FBI alerts of a possible Iranian drone attack on California following the US-Iran war meant police dogs were visible on the red carpet and helicopters circled overhead throughout the night. The Revolution Is Already Happening
When the 2023 Hollywood writers' and actors' strikes placed AI at the centre of the most significant labour dispute the entertainment industry had seen in decades, many observers assumed the debate would resolve cleanly — either AI would be limited by contract or it would be unleashed. The reality of 2026 is, predictably, messier and more interesting than either extreme. AI has become deeply embedded in the entertainment industry's production infrastructure, its marketing, its content recommendation systems, and increasingly its creative process itself. And the industry is still actively negotiating what that means for the humans at its centre.
A landmark WrapPRO research report published in January 2026 identified AI integration as the single most defining trend in the entertainment industry this year. "Breakthroughs in generative video are transforming production workflows," the report found, "even as audiences and creatives remain uneasy about authenticity, labor impacts, and AI's environmental footprint." That tension — between the efficiency AI offers and the humanity it potentially displaces — is the defining story of entertainment in 2026.
How Studios Are Using AI Right Now
The most widespread current application of AI in Hollywood is in pre-production and post-production workflows. AI tools are being used to generate concept art and storyboards at a fraction of traditional cost. Visual effects studios are using AI-powered compositing and de-aging tools that would have required hundreds of hours of human VFX work five years ago. AI dubbing and lip-sync technology — which can translate an actor's dialogue into any language while maintaining the appearance of speaking that language naturally — is transforming how global content is distributed, with Netflix reporting that AI-dubbed content has significantly higher completion rates than subtitle-only versions in non-English-speaking markets including India.
In music, AI composition tools are now routinely used in the production of film scores, advertising music, and streaming playlist content. The controversy over AI-generated music that mimics specific artists' vocal styles has prompted several high-profile legal challenges. In March 2026, the US Copyright Office released updated guidance on AI-generated content — determining that works created entirely by AI are not eligible for copyright protection, but that human-AI collaborative works may qualify depending on the degree of human creative input.
The Creator Economy and AI: Threat or Tool?
For independent content creators — YouTubers, podcasters, Instagramers, and TikTok creators — AI presents a more ambiguous picture. On one hand, AI tools dramatically lower the cost of producing high-quality content: AI can write scripts, edit video, generate thumbnails, and optimise posting schedules. Creators who once needed teams to compete with major studios can now produce professional-quality content solo. On the other hand, the same AI tools that empower individual creators also enable the mass production of AI-generated content that floods platforms, making it harder for any individual creator to stand out.
The WrapPRO report identifies the "dissolution of boundaries between Hollywood and the creator economy" as one of the six key trends defining 2026, noting that studios are increasingly working with YouTube and TikTok creators as both marketers and talent, while social platforms are professionalising and moving into the living room through smart TV apps.

Indian viewers are experiencing AI's entertainment impact primarily through two channels: personalised content recommendation (Netflix and Amazon Prime's AI-driven "what to watch next" engines are becoming increasingly accurate at predicting Indian viewing preferences across languages and genres) and AI dubbing of Korean and Western content into Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, and other Indian languages, dramatically expanding the addressable audience for global content.
